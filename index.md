#### I improve complex operations, programs, and workflows by understanding how people, processes, resources, and information interact—and redesigning the systems that connect them.


I am drawn to the places where good work gets harder than it needs to be—where complex systems, limited resources, competing priorities, and human realities collide. My interests span science, healthcare, education, and workforce development, but the underlying question is the same: how can we build systems that work better for the people inside them and produce better outcomes for the people they serve?

I have always approached problems through a systems lens, and my training in biochemistry provided a rigorous environment to apply and refine that perspective. Over time, this focus extended beyond experimental work to how laboratory systems actually operate: where they break down, how they can be improved, and how better structure can support both the people doing the work and the outcomes they are trying to achieve.

I use systems-based approaches such as continuous improvement and Lean methodologies to identify root causes, reduce inefficiencies, and strengthen reliability. I am particularly motivated by opportunities to turn complex work and ambitious goals into practical, well-functioning systems that are easier to navigate, more consistent, and better aligned with the needs of the people and communities they serve.
